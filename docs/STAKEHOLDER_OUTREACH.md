## Shareable overview

Attachment: [SteadRoute Stakeholder Overview PDF](outreach/SteadRoute_Stakeholder_Overview.pdf).

This PDF preserves the previously approved planning-stage wording. Before reusing it after implementation progresses, check that its current-status statements still match actual progress. Do not imply that replies or partnerships have been established.

SteadRoute is a capstone application in the planning stage, designed to help people preparing for release from incarceration understand transitional housing options and participate meaningfully in placement planning. Its initial focus is Arizona reentry housing and sober living programs that accept people returning from incarceration.

The project grew from Amber Goding's observations during seven years incarcerated at ASPC-Perryville, where she witnessed women struggle to find housing information relevant to their needs and goals.

Participants would search by destination, budget, and relevant requirements, then compare up to three programs. Reviewed listings would explain eligibility, recurring and upfront costs, employment rules, activity restrictions, transportation, and support services. Missing information would be clearly identified.

Verified providers would maintain their listings through a separate workspace, with publication review and scheduled confirmation reminders. Optional AI-assisted drafting would help authorized providers organize information from public program pages; human review would remain required. Participants would have no AI interface.

Planned supporting workflows include staff-authorized inquiries and applications, participant approval of information shared, and coordination among providers and authorized correctional staff. SteadRoute would support existing placement processes and record required approvals without replacing DOC or provider decision-making authority.

SteadRoute has a documented product plan and early screen designs. No DOC, tablet-vendor, or provider partnerships have been established. The intended tablet experience is a dedicated, approved application; its delivery method and operational requirements still need validation.

We are seeking brief conversations with housing providers and correctional stakeholders to understand current practices, information gaps, staff workload, and whether the proposed workflow would be useful and practical.

Contact: Amber Goding

## Housing provider conversation questions

Allow about 20 minutes. Start with the current process before showing the proposed product. Ask permission before recording or attributing comments by name, and keep examples anonymous.

Opening: "Thank you for speaking with me. I'm developing SteadRoute to help people preparing for release understand and compare transitional housing. I'd like to learn how your process works and where my assumptions may be wrong. Please keep examples anonymous."

1. Could you walk me through a recent application from someone preparing for release? How did they find your program, communicate with you, and receive a decision?
2. What information do applicants most often lack or misunderstand? Which misunderstandings create problems for applicants or your team?
3. What determines whether someone is a good fit for your program? Which requirements are fixed, and which need an individual conversation?
4. How do you currently share and update program information? Who handles changes to costs, eligibility, rules, and application availability?
5. Looking at this proposed workflow, what would help your team, and what would create extra work or concern? What would prevent you from maintaining a listing or receiving inquiries through SteadRoute?
6. What would you need to see before considering a small trial? Would you be open to reviewing an early version, or recommending someone else I should speak with?

## DOC staff conversation questions

Opening: "Thank you for your time. My experience at Perryville motivated SteadRoute, but I want to understand the staff perspective and the requirements I may have missed. I'm seeking feedback, not access to individual records. Please keep examples anonymous."

1. Could you walk me through how transitional housing is identified and approved for someone preparing for release? Who handles each step, and who has final authority?
2. Where can participants research options, suggest programs, or express preferences today? What limits their ability to do so?
3. Where does housing planning most often become difficult or delayed? What information or communication is usually missing?
4. Looking at SteadRoute's proposed search, comparison, inquiry, and application workflow, which parts would be useful or impractical? What could add unnecessary staff work or conflict with existing procedures?
5. Who would need to review a tool like this before participants could use it? What technology, security, communication, or recordkeeping requirements should I investigate first?
6. What would be a realistic next step for validating the idea? Could you review an early version or introduce me to the appropriate release-planning, Community Supervision, or technology contact?
